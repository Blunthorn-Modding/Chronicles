# House goals: a pool of ambitions

[Workbook](README.md) · [Setting](SETTING.md)

> Worldbuilding draft for discussion, not implemented content. These fifty goals give characters motivations for political roleplay, without scores or fixed end states.

Draw **two different goals per character**. Goals can be shared by different
Houses. Check the author-only eligibility table before drawing either goal.

## Crown, influence and power

### G01 — Claim the Crown

Your House wants the Crown. You believe it can unite the kingdom and bring your family lasting power.

### G02 — A ruler who respects the provinces

You want a ruler who protects local rights and respects the different customs of the provinces.

### G03 — Patronage at court

Your House wants an appointment from the next ruler, giving your family a recognised place at court.

### G04 — The power behind the throne

You want the next ruler to value your advice and hear your House before making important decisions.

### G05 — A ruler who listens

You want the Crown to consult the Council on major decisions, so every House has a voice.

### G06 — A strong hand

You want the next ruler to have stronger emergency powers, because a divided realm cannot always respond quickly.

### G07 — Support on your terms

Your House wants a candidate to publicly promise lower taxes in return for its support.

### G08 — Free trade before the Crown

Your House wants the Council to protect free trade between the provinces, regardless of who wins the Crown.

## Power and public decisions

### G09 — Public appointments

You want important Crown appointments made openly, because the kingdom should know who receives office and why.

### G10 — Merit in office

You want public offices filled for ability and service, rather than awarded solely for political loyalty.

### G11 — A voice before decisions

Your House wants proposals affecting the whole kingdom discussed by the Council before the Crown makes a commitment.

### G12 — Limits on emergency powers

You want clear limits on emergency powers, because urgent decisions should not give a ruler unchecked authority.

### G13 — Public spending

You want the Crown to explain how public money is spent, so the Houses can challenge waste and favouritism.

### G14 — Protect local decisions

Your House wants local communities to retain control over their own affairs, even when central rules seem more convenient.

### G15 — A common relief fund

You want the kingdom to establish a shared relief fund for communities facing hunger or other hardship.

### G16 — Review public decisions

You want the Council to review major public decisions, so mistakes and unfair treatment can be challenged openly.

## Reform and privilege

### G17 — The cost of a closed market

Your House opposes commercial monopolies because exclusive privileges can enrich a few and leave everyone else dependent.

### G18 — A profitable exception

Your House wants an exclusive commercial privilege, believing protected trade can reward enterprise and support its ambitions.

### G19 — Roads for all

You want low tolls, because expensive travel can isolate communities and make ordinary commerce harder for everyone.

### G20 — Let the toll pay for the road

You want tolls to fund road and bridge maintenance, so travellers contribute to keeping routes safe and usable.

### G21 — Fairness across the provinces

You want local privileges examined for unfair exclusions, even when reform means changing an arrangement some communities value.

### G22 — Custom against uniformity

Your House wants local customs protected from uniform rules, because different communities should retain their own ways of life.

### G23 — Gambling without shame

Your House wants public gambling treated as respectable business, free from the social disgrace attached to it.

### G24 — The price of an office

You want appointments to public office kept separate from payments, because government should not look like a marketplace.

## Agreements, debts and concessions

### G25 — Mutual protection

Your House wants an agreement with another House to support each other if either becomes involved in a conflict.

### G26 — Public agreements

Your House wants trade agreements made public, so other Houses can judge whether promises are being kept.

### G27 — A private commercial deal

Your House wants the freedom to make private commercial agreements, believing confidential negotiation can help trade and investment.

### G28 — The debt of gratitude

You want to help another House with a present problem and earn goodwill your family can call upon later.

### G29 — A coalition for relief

Your House wants other Houses to support a common programme that provides grain to communities facing hunger.

### G30 — The unsold vote

Your House believes its Council voice is a public trust and refuses to exchange its judgment for personal advantage.

### G31 — Fair terms for trade

Your House wants an agreement with another House that improves trade while protecting the interests of both parties.

### G32 — A compact for the road

Your House wants to cooperate with another House to make travel and exchange along a shared route more dependable.

## Foreign policy and conflict

### G33 — Credit across the border

Your House wants access to credit from a neighbouring country, so merchants and families have more choices for funding.

### G34 — The danger of foreign credit

You want scrutiny of Crown borrowing from neighbouring countries, because debt could give foreign powers influence over the kingdom.

### G35 — Open foreign markets

Your House wants trade with neighbouring countries to flourish, bringing merchants new customers and communities a wider supply of goods.

### G36 — The cost of reliance

Your House wants the kingdom to diversify its foreign trade, because relying on one neighbour leaves essential supplies vulnerable.

### G37 — Peace at the border

Your House wants disputes with neighbouring kingdoms settled through negotiation, because border conflict would harm families on both sides.

### G38 — Ready for the worst

Your House wants stronger border defences, believing the kingdom should be prepared even when its neighbours are peaceful.

### G39 — Neighbours before enemies

Your House wants cross-border communities and their trade protected, because political tension should not sever their everyday connections.

### G40 — Who speaks for the realm?

Your House wants the Council to discuss foreign commitments openly before anyone makes promises on the kingdom's behalf.

## Reputation, loyalty and ethics

### G41 — The trusted mediator

Your House wants to be trusted as a mediator when disagreements between other Houses threaten cooperation in the Council.

### G42 — Give every House a voice

Your House wants every delegation heard, especially when a large coalition risks overlooking the concerns of others.

### G43 — Loyalty with conditions

Your House values loyalty but believes it should be earned through shared principles and honourable conduct, not demanded by rank.

### G44 — A reputation for candour

Your House wants a reputation for speaking candidly, even when an uncomfortable truth threatens a convenient political agreement.

### G45 — Trust beyond reputation

You want your House judged by its actions at this Council, rather than by its public reputation.

### G46 — A voice for the forgotten

Your House wants the settlement to protect people without a seat at the Council, especially communities facing hardship.

### G47 — The useful rival

Your House wants a lasting working relationship with another delegation, even where your political interests and convictions differ.

### G48 — Fairness before advantage

Your House wants public decisions to treat people fairly, even when an unfair arrangement might benefit your family.

### G49 — The public cost of a bargain

Your House wants political deals to disclose their public consequences, including who benefits and who bears the cost.

### G50 — Mercy and accountability

Your House wants a settlement that holds people accountable while allowing those who admit mistakes a chance at mercy.
