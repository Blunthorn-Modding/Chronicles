# The Last Council — shared setting and writing rules

> **Worldbuilding draft · 22 November 2026 · Not implemented.**
> This is an editable proposal for the next content set. It does not replace the
> beta's configuration or change a played world. Read [the index](README.md) first.

## The situation everyone knows

The monarch has died without an acknowledged successor. Under the Interregnum
Charter, the noble Houses represented at the royal palace govern collectively
until they elect a ruler. Their decisions still matter if the throne remains
vacant. The Chancellor conducts proceedings and represents no House.

The realm was assembled through inheritance, conquest and negotiated submission.
Its provinces retain different customs and privileges. Every House governs a
mixture of people and livelihoods; a grain exporter also has craftsmen, tenants
and minor trade. No House owns the realm's entire supply of a necessary good.
Wealth, public reputation and legal status do not make a family inherently
virtuous, villainous, monarchist or rebellious.

The sixteen proposed Houses are Harrowmere, Brackenholt, Morcant, Rooke, Tern,
Marwick, Keld, Talvern, Ormont, Bellayne, Veyne, Avenor, Caldren, Durnholt, Selwyn
and Wrenford. These are working names. The map's fifteen main spaces and three
additional spaces are capacity for visiting delegations, not a requirement to
play all sixteen at once. That map interpretation remains provisional.

The palace is protected neutral ground. Home estates, towns and most events
described here are elsewhere in the realm; no additional rooms, NPCs, maps or
physical props are required merely because they appear in a written account.
Absent Houses remain part of the setting but cannot supply signatures or votes
and must never be necessary to complete an assigned goal.

## What changes between games

| Layer | How this draft treats it |
|---|---|
| House identity | Fixed public history, reputation, lands and broad influence. |
| Private House information | Draw exactly one of that House's five entries. These are alternatives, not five simultaneous truths. |
| General secret | Draw one from the separate fifty-entry pool; use each entry at most once in a game. |
| House goal | Draw motivations from the fifty-entry pool after checking the actual cast, political state and required background. The number issued remains an organizer choice. |
| Knowledge | Independently determine which other Houses learn each selected private entry. Zero, one or several observers are possible; owners receive no exposure indicator. |
| Complications | Choose eligible entries at concealed points during Council meetings, before that meeting's succession vote. No prescribed session sequence. |

Private House information may be useful strength, limited capacity, concealed
dependence or an unusual opportunity. A general secret may concern a completely
different side of the family. Goals need not follow the public business or
reward exposing the assigned secret. Players choose how to use their leverage.

A public **reputation** can be misleading. Established geography and actual
private facts must remain consistent. A celebrated grain supplier may secretly
buy its grain abroad; two private entries cannot disagree about whether a
particular debt has already been repaid. Resolve conflicts by choosing a
compatible entry before publication, never by silently rewriting issued papers.
Every learned copy of a private entry must carry the same facts and numbers.

## Common institutions and laws

These short definitions are public background, supplied to every delegation.
They create matters that players can argue about without inventing missing
history during the evening. All non-constitutional policies below can be
addressed through an ordinary Council motion.

| Institution or rule | Public starting position |
|---|---|
| **Interregnum Charter** | Each participating loyal House has its normal Council rights. Succession and ballot rules are those of the game, not rewritten by a free-text motion. |
| **Local charters** | Towns and provinces hold recognised privileges. New exemptions, changes or confirmations can be recorded by Council decree. |
| **Open Roads Ordinance** | Existing customary tolls remain lawful. A new toll or an increase needs Council approval. |
| **Customs declaration** | Foreign commercial payments and imported goods must be declared. Receiving foreign money is not itself treason; concealing it is an offence. |
| **Public works warrants** | Bridges, roads, harbours and relief programmes may receive Council authority and pledged support. A warrant grants a political commission, not an extra vote or control of a player. |
| **Registered claims** | Debts, grants and supply promises can be recorded or guaranteed by the Crown. The Council may recognise, settle or forgive a specified claim; it cannot compel a House to sign a voluntary treaty. |
| **Office and favour** | Trade licences, court appointments and commercial advantages must be disclosed when obtained for payment. Undisclosed payment for an official favour is an offence. |
| **Gambling houses** | Public gambling establishments require a licence; concealing ownership evades that rule. Household games and private wagers are lawful. |
| **Seals and correspondence** | Fabricating a royal warrant, falsely claiming royal authority or opening a sealed official dispatch without authority is an offence. Ordinary criticism of the Crown is lawful. |
| **Relief endowments** | Assets legally donated for public relief must serve that purpose. The Council may authorise a stated, publicly recorded change of use. |

No entry silently changes these laws. A secret describing an offence states
which act occurred. A proposal to legalise or pardon something states its scope.
Historical wrongdoing does not automatically strip Council eligibility, resolve
a ballot or impose a mechanical penalty. Characters may argue for political
consequences through the supported procedures.

## Foreign and outside actors

- **Rheven:** a neighbouring maritime realm trading in grain, shipping and
  credit. Trade is lawful; concealed arrangements and political concessions can
  create controversy. Rheven is neither inherently friendly nor hostile.
- **Vask:** a neighbouring realm sharing land routes and border communities.
  Border trade, negotiated access and military tension are all plausible; no
  war is assumed at the start.
- **The Free Carriers:** independent merchants who can offer transport contracts.
- **The Chartered Towns' Assembly:** a collective petitioner for town privileges.
- **The Royal Treasury, customs clerks and relief trustees:** institutions whose
  written accounts can create questions for the Council.

Rheven and Vask both exist in this proposed fiction. A realm can remain part
of a House's history without being an active diplomatic topic that evening.
If an adaptation removes Vask from the fictional world altogether, revise or
omit Talvern's public background and its relevant private entries as well.
Leaving Vask out of active diplomacy does not erase the border or its people.

These actors exist through the information supplied in the documents. Goals
cannot require an actor to attend, answer questions or agree to a new bargain.
A complication's written offer includes its terms and says what Council record
constitutes acceptance. The Chancellor need not improvise an ambassador.
Any foreign-policy entry requires the relevant realm to exist in that game's
setup; otherwise exclude it before drawing.

Names used in outside reports are **Crown Quay** (a royal harbour), **Millford
Road** (a public road), **Eastgate Orchard** (a royal estate), **Long Hall
Storehouse** (a public relief building), **Bell Market** and **Reedmarket**
(chartered towns), and **Hearthford** (a divided border community). The **Lantern
Trust** and **Roads Trust** are outside charitable bodies, and the **Goldworkers'
Guild** is an outside lender. These places and institutions require no
additional physical builds. They do not give a player House an exclusive claim
unless an actual agreement or Council decision establishes one.

Amounts in **silver** are narrative accounts that establish a bargain's scale.
They are not a new inventory currency or a compulsory estate budget. A player
can negotiate a repayment, guarantee or contribution through roleplay and
political commitments; the documents do not require obtaining stacks of items.

## Goals as roleplay motivations

Goals are things a character wants and pressures they can act on, not scored
tasks. Players decide what progress, compromise, sacrifice or accomplishment
means for their character. We do not need exhaustive success conditions, a
victory checklist or a Chancellor's judgement of who completed the most goals.
Possible approaches are invitations rather than the only acceptable routes.

1. Every goal supplies a reason to care, the background needed to discuss it
   fairly, and something the character can meaningfully pursue during the
   evening. A decree, agreement, new relationship or convincing compromise may
   all matter. Future construction or deliveries can remain imagined stakes.
2. A goal may be difficult or opposed. It cannot require an absent House, an
   unknown relative, an unavailable witness, a specific random complication or
   information the recipient was never given.
3. A crown-related ambition needs an appropriate participating candidate or a
   credible route into candidacy before the slate closes. No goal implies an
   existing alliance, guarantees a vote or changes candidacy. An unsuccessful
   claimant can still have an interesting and satisfying political journey.
4. A target chosen from the cast is an eligible **present participating House**,
   distinct from the owner unless stated otherwise. Named targets must be
   resolved once and then preserved. Policy targets need no shared secret.
5. Where a prompt mentions other Houses, use a number appropriate to the actual
   cast. Numbers can suggest the scale of an ambition without becoming a score.
6. Give no House mutually exclusive mandatory goals. Across Houses, opposing
   goals are desirable; everyone need not succeed. Refusal to negotiate is play,
   not a validation failure.
7. General Council decisions remain binding political records. House treaties
   are voluntary and breakable; witnessing them does not make them Council law.
8. Political records preserve what happened, not a pass/fail verdict on a
   player's ambitions. This draft does not introduce automatic scoring,
   economic simulation, extra offices with mechanical powers, compulsory
   combat or compulsory poisoning.

## Complications and the Chancellor's surprise

The author can read the possible pool; the Chancellor should not see the live
selection, future targets or countdown before delivery. A written complication
provides its own facts, a public opening message and possible responses. The
Chancellor presents it and conducts the discussion without choosing a winner.

Deliver only in an active Council meeting at a safe boundary between ballots.
A late arrival creates time for discussion before a succession ballot. Preserve
existing votes and signed terms. Do not force a vote, signature, war, rebellion,
exposure of a private cast entry or a physical world change. Eligibility must
exclude contradictory offers and proposals made obsolete by earlier decisions.

Complications may create new outside developments. They must not retrospectively
invent crimes, relationships, depleted reserves or promises for a player House.
Reports and accusations must distinguish what is proven from what is alleged.

## Scope of this work

All entries are original fictional proposals. Historical and literary research
in [Sources](SOURCES.md) supplies patterns, not copied characters or factual
claims about living people. The content is not a historical simulation.

**Implementation is postponed.** No scenario defaults, configuration, map or
mod code is being changed. Content and political compatibility belong to The
Last Council. Its future draw will need to distinguish the two private pools
and validate goals against the cast. Existing Chronicle pool/template services
can be reused; this is not yet a promise that the design is JSON-only or that
every new dependency rule already exists. No Chronicle Core change is proposed
by this worldbuilding draft.
